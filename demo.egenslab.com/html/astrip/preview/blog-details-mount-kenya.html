Mount Kenya Trekking Guide: Routes, Fitness Tips & Best Camps

© 2026 • Rasset Adventures

Rising majestically above Kenya's central highlands, Mount Kenya is one of Africa's most spectacular trekking destinations. With dramatic peaks, alpine lakes, glaciers, unique Afro-alpine vegetation, and breathtaking panoramic views, climbing Africa's second-highest mountain is an adventure that combines challenge, beauty, and achievement.

Whether you're an experienced mountaineer aiming for the summit or a nature enthusiast seeking a scenic high-altitude trek, Mount Kenya offers routes and experiences suitable for a wide range of fitness levels. This guide covers everything you need to know—from choosing the best route to preparing physically and selecting the ideal camps for your journey.

WHY TREK MOUNT KENYA?

Standing at 5,199 meters (17,057 feet) above sea level, Mount Kenya is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of East Africa's most rewarding mountain adventures.

  • Spectacular mountain scenery
  • Diverse ecosystems and wildlife
  • Crystal-clear alpine lakes
  • Unique giant lobelias and senecios
  • Less crowded trails than Kilimanjaro
  • Exceptional sunrise views from the summit area
  • Opportunities for technical and non-technical climbs

UNDERSTANDING THE MAIN PEAKS

Mount Kenya has three principal peaks:

Batian (5,199m)

The highest peak, requiring advanced technical rock-climbing skills.

Nelion (5,188m)

The second-highest peak, also requiring technical climbing experience.

Point Lenana (4,985m)

The most accessible trekking summit and the goal for most visitors. Point Lenana does not require technical climbing equipment, making it achievable for reasonably fit trekkers with proper acclimatization.

BEST MOUNT KENYA TREKKING ROUTES

1. Sirimon Route (Most Popular)

The Sirimon Route approaches the mountain from the northwest and is widely regarded as the most scenic and gradual ascent route.

  • Excellent acclimatization profile
  • Beautiful landscapes
  • Gentle gradients
  • High summit success rates
  • Suitable for first-time high-altitude trekkers

Typical Camps: Old Moses Camp (Judmaier Camp), Shipton's Camp. Recommended Duration: 3–4 days.

2. Chogoria Route (Most Scenic)

Many experienced trekkers consider the Chogoria Route the most beautiful way to explore Mount Kenya.

  • Lake Ellis
  • Lake Michaelson
  • Gorges Valley
  • Waterfalls and dramatic cliffs

Typical Camps: Lake Ellis Camp, Mintos Camp. Recommended Duration: 4–5 days.

3. Naro Moru Route (Fastest Route)

The Naro Moru Route is the quickest and most direct path to Point Lenana.

  • Shortest route
  • Popular with experienced trekkers
  • Faster summit access

Challenges: Steeper ascent and lower acclimatization time. Typical Camps: Met Station, Mackinder's Camp. Recommended Duration: 4–5 days.

BEST CAMPS ON MOUNT KENYA

Old Moses Camp (3,300m)

Located on the Sirimon Route, Old Moses Camp serves as an excellent first-night acclimatization stop.

Shipton's Camp (4,200m)

One of Mount Kenya's most famous camps, located near Point Lenana, known for spectacular mountain views and excellent acclimatization.

Mintos Camp (4,300m)

Situated on the Chogoria Route, Mintos Camp offers breathtaking sunrise and sunset views and quiet alpine surroundings.

Mackinder's Camp (4,200m)

The primary high-altitude camp on the Naro Moru Route, convenient for summit attempts.

FITNESS PREPARATION FOR MOUNT KENYA

How Fit Do You Need to Be?

Reaching Point Lenana does not require technical climbing skills, but good physical fitness significantly improves your experience.

You should comfortably be able to walk 6–8 hours per day, climb hills carrying a daypack, and handle multiple consecutive trekking days.

Recommended Training Plan

Begin training at least 6–8 weeks before your trek.

Cardiovascular Exercise

Focus on running, cycling, swimming, brisk walking, and stair climbing. Aim for 3–5 sessions per week.

Hiking Practice

Whenever possible, hike local trails with a light backpack to simulate trekking conditions.

Strength Training

Strengthen legs, core, lower back, and shoulders using squats, lunges, step-ups, planks, and deadlifts.

Altitude & Acclimatization Tips

  • Ascend gradually
  • Stay well hydrated
  • Avoid excessive alcohol before the trek
  • Walk slowly and steadily
  • Follow guide instructions carefully

WHAT TO PACK

Essential Clothing

  • Waterproof jacket
  • Warm fleece layers
  • Thermal base layers
  • Trekking trousers
  • Gloves and warm hat
  • Moisture-wicking socks

Trekking Equipment

  • Daypack
  • Trekking poles
  • Headlamp
  • Sunglasses
  • Water bottles or hydration system
  • Sunscreen and lip balm

BEST TIME TO CLIMB MOUNT KENYA

January–March and June–October offer the clearest skies and most stable trekking conditions. April–May and November are rainy seasons and can make trails muddy.
